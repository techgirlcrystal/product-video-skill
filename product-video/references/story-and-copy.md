# Story structure and on-screen copy

These videos have no narration. The captions and the motion carry everything, so every word on screen has to earn its place.

## The arc (60–80s for a site/landing video, 30–45s for social)

| Beat | Length | Look | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 4–6s | dark | The single most painful **real** number the product surfaces, counting up. "449 contacts are stuck." Real item names drift behind it, blurred. |
| 2. Why it hurts | 3–5s | dark, warm glow | Make the pain concrete: 1–2 cards naming the real culprits with status pills (DRAFT, PAUSED, BROKEN), plus one quiet line ("Nobody would ever know. Unless you looked."). |
| 3. Brand reveal | 4–5s | dark, accent glow | Logo tile + wordmark + one-line promise (e.g. "See everything inside any GHL account."). |
| 4. Features | 8–11s each, 3–5 of them | light, split | One feature per scene: eyebrow label, 2-beat headline, one-line subhead, an animated card rebuilt from real data, 1–2 floating stat chips. |
| 5. Cinematic moment | 7–9s | dark | Something big and visual: a whole-account network map growing outward, or a 3D-tilted browser window panning over the real report. |
| 6. End card | 5–6s | dark | Logo, tagline, the URL in a glowing pill. **URL only** unless the user asks for another CTA. |

Order the features by how much they would make a buyer say "I need that", not by how the product's tabs are ordered. Put the feature most tied to the hook first, so the story pays off the pain straight away.

## Why the hook matters

A feature tour ("here's tab 1, tab 2") doesn't stop anyone scrolling. A painful, specific number does, because every buyer privately worries they have that problem. Then the rest of the video explains how the product finds it. Pick the number from the product's own findings or summary tiles, not a marketing claim.

## Headline rules

- **Two beats, the second in the accent color:** "Know what breaks **before you delete it.**" / "Every workflow. **Drawn out.**" / "A health score. **And the fixes that raise it.**"
- Headlines are outcomes for the viewer, not feature names. "See where every contact is sitting" beats "Workflow enrollment analytics".
- Subheads are one plain-English sentence, about 15 words max.
- Never implementation details (no "JSON", "API", "export engine").
- Floating chips over a scrolling screenshot must be **true of everything that scrolls past**. Use general truths ("WHERE · the exact screen it lives on"), not a specific finding that might sit next to a different one on screen.
- Leave out anything the user says not to mention (for example "we don't need to say it exports to Excel"). Check the whole script for it, not just the scene they pointed at.

## Honesty rules

- Every number, name and diagram comes from the product's real output (an example/demo account is fine and usually ideal, since it has no customer PII).
- Don't claim what the product doesn't do. If a feature needs two runs to show (for example "changes since last export") and the demo has one, leave it out.
- Tell the user plainly which scenes are weak and whether the length is too long. A tighter video converts better, and a vague "looks great" doesn't help them ship.

## Timing conventions

- Scene crossfade 0.45s, where the new scene fades **in over** the old one (never fade both at once: light-to-light cuts dip to gray).
- Stagger element entrances 0.2–0.6s apart: eyebrow → headline → sub → card → chips.
- Count-ups take about 1.4s with ease-out; bars about 1.4s with ease-in-out, each row 0.3s after the last.
- Give each scene about 1.5s of "hold" after its last element lands, so it can be read.

## Short reels (15s)

A reel is not a mini overview. It's **one idea**, fast:

| Beat | Length | Pick from the full video |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 2.5–3s | The pain-number count-up, or the most striking visual (network map) |
| Payoff | 4–5.5s ×1–2 | The feature that answers that hook: bars filling, a diagram drawing, the report pan |
| End card | 4s | Logo + tagline + URL |

Good angles are usually one per headline feature: **the pain** (hook → problem cards → the feature that finds it), **the visuals** (map → diagram → dependencies), **the report** (hook → score → report pan).

Honest guidance to give the user:
- The reel that opens on a problem converts best with cold audiences. Post it first and use it for paid ads.
- A reel that's pure visuals (no pain) is for warm audiences and retargeting.
- At 3–5s a scene, viewers read the headline and the numbers, not the subheads. Repeat the hook in the post caption.
- For ads, test a different end line per reel ("See your stuck contacts →") instead of the same tagline everywhere.
